Spec 20.1 Formulae from data

1. Empirical & Molecular Formulae

🎯 4 Key Learning Goals:
  • From combustion analysis / % composition: convert % → moles → simplest ratio → empirical formula.
  • Use the mass-spec molecular ion peak ($M^+$) to get the molar mass.
  • Molecular formula = (empirical formula)$_n$ where $n = M / M_{empirical}$.
  • Recognise common fragments: $M-15$ ($\text{CH}_3^+$), $M-29$ ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5^+$ or $\text{CHO}^+$), $M-45$ ($\text{COOH}^+$).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Show the mole-ratio working. A fragment at $M-45$ strongly suggests a carboxylic acid ($-\text{COOH}$).

Spec 20.1 IR spectroscopy

2. Infrared — Identify the Functional Group

🎯 4 Key Learning Goals:
  • $\text{C=O}$ strong sharp ~1680–1750 cm⁻¹ (aldehyde/ketone/acid/ester/amide).
  • $\text{O–H}$: alcohol ~3200–3600 (broad); carboxylic acid ~2500–3300 (very broad, overlaps C–H).
  • $\text{N–H}$ ~3300–3500; $\text{C}\equiv\text{N}$ ~2200–2250; $\text{C=C}$ ~1620–1680.
  • Combine the C=O presence/absence with O–H/N–H to pin down the family.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

A broad O–H plus a C=O = carboxylic acid. A C=O with no O–H = aldehyde or ketone (distinguish by the aldehyde C–H ~2700–2850 or by Tollens').

Spec 20.1 NMR deduction

3. $^{13}\text{C}$ & $^1\text{H}$ NMR — Build the Structure

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • $^{13}\text{C}$ NMR: number of peaks = number of carbon environments.
  • $^1\text{H}$ NMR: number of peaks = proton environments; integration = relative H count.
  • Splitting ($n+1$ rule): neighbours on the adjacent carbon.
  • Chemical shift tables: e.g. $\text{R–CH}_3$ ~0.9, $\text{R–CH}_2$ ~1.3, $\text{CH}_3\text{CO}$ ~2.1, $\text{O–CH}$ ~3.5–4.5, aldehyde H ~9.5–10, acid H ~10–12.
  • Assemble fragments (from shifts + splitting + integration) into a structure consistent with the molecular formula.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

An exchangeable proton (O–H, N–H) appears as a singlet and disappears on D₂O shake. Check your structure against all the data, not just one peak.

Spec 20.2 Chain extension

4. Grignard Reagents — Extend the Chain

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Form RMgX from haloalkane + Mg in anhydrous dry ether, reflux.
  • + CO₂ (dry ice) then dil HCl → carboxylic acid (+1C).
  • + methanal then dil HCl → 1° alcohol (+1C).
  • + aldehyde then dil HCl → 2° alcohol; + ketone then dil HCl → 3° alcohol.
  • Know why the ether must be dry (water destroys RMgX).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

A 3° alcohol can only come from Grignard + ketone (Cycle 2 can't make it). This is a classic "which route?" exam answer. Paper cue: WCH15 Jun 2026 Q5a — (CH₃)₂CHCH₂MgBr gives a specific alcohol; the wrong Grignard always gives the wrong alcohol, so match the alkyl group to the target.

Spec 20.3 i – ii Prediction & route design

5. Predicting Properties & Planning up to 4 Steps

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Predict properties of unfamiliar compounds from their functional groups (solubility, b.p., reactivity).
  • Plan a route of up to 4 steps, recalling familiar reactions and using unfamiliar ones given data.
  • Count carbons first — decide whether you need a chain-extension/shortening step.
  • Map functional groups step-by-step using the cycle map.
  • Remember overall yield = $y_1 \times y_2 \times y_3$ — fewer steps = higher yield.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Write reagents AND conditions for every step — marks are split between them. See the Exam Walkthroughs tab for the full method. Overall yield = product of step yields (y₁ × y₂ × y₃) — a multi-step route is capped by the weakest step. Paper cue: WCH15 Jun 2026 Q11b — overall yield 57% (only-step-1-yield answers lose the mark).

Spec 20.3 iii – iv Practical & safety

6. Practical Procedures & Risk Control

🎯 4 Key Learning Goals:
  • Choose the right setup: reflux (to heat without losing volatile reagents), distillation (to collect a volatile product).
  • Purify by washing (water / Na₂CO₃ to remove acid), solvent extraction, drying (anhydrous salt), then distillation.
  • Check purity by melting/boiling point (sharp = pure).
  • Identify control measures from hazard data (fume cupboard for toxic/volatile, no flames for flammable, gloves for corrosive).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

"Why reflux?" = to prevent loss of volatile reactants/products while heating. "Why Na₂CO₃ wash?" = to neutralise/remove excess acid.

Spec 20.4 Core Practical 16

7. Core Practical 16 — Preparation of Aspirin

🎯 4 Key Learning Goals:
  • 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid) + ethanoic anhydride → aspirin (2-ethanoyloxybenzoic acid) + ethanoic acid.
  • Conditions: warm (~60 °C), acid catalyst; ethanoic anhydride is preferred over ethanoyl chloride (less violent, no HCl).
  • Purify by recrystallisation; check purity by melting point (pure aspirin ~135 °C).
  • Calculate the percentage yield from the limiting reagent.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

This is an esterification of a phenol — the –OH is on the benzene ring. Know why anhydride is used instead of the acyl chloride.

Spec 20.5 Purification techniques

8. Preparation & Purification Techniques

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Reflux vs distillation vs steam distillation — know when each is used.
  • Washing with water / Na₂CO₃ in a separating funnel; solvent extraction.
  • Drying with an anhydrous salt (e.g. MgSO₄) then filtering.
  • Recrystallisation for solids (choose a solvent, hot filter, cool, collect).
  • Melting point (solid) and boiling point (liquid) as purity checks.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Steam distillation isolates heat-sensitive, immiscible-with-water organics (e.g. plant oils) below their normal boiling point.

🎯 The universal 4-step method for ANY synthesis question
1 · Count carbonsStart vs target. More? → chain extension (KCN/HCN/Grignard/Friedel–Crafts). Fewer? → iodoform (−1C). Same? → cycles only.
2 · Map functional groupsWrite the FG of the start and target. What must change, and in what order?
3 · Pick the cycle per stepEach step = one cycle reaction. C1 hydrocarbons, C2 redox, C3 acid/derivatives, C4 aromatic.
4 · Reagents + conditionsMarks are split between reagents and conditions. Write both, every step. Check for racemic/chirality notes.

⚠️ Master Trap Checklist the marks you must not drop ▼
  • Ethanolic KOH = elimination; aqueous KOH = substitution. The solvent decides.
  • Distil to stop at the aldehyde; reflux to reach the carboxylic acid.
  • KCN needs ethanol (not water) — water gives the alcohol instead.
  • LiAlH₄ reduces acids; NaBH₄ does not.
  • 3° amines can't form amides (no N–H to eliminate).
  • 2 mol of amine/NH₃ per acyl chloride — one neutralises the HCl.
  • Diazonium: 0–10 °C or it decomposes to phenol + N₂.
  • Nitration: 55 °C — hotter gives dinitration.
  • Grignard: anhydrous dry ether — water destroys RMgX.
  • Alkaline ester hydrolysis gives the SALT, not the acid; acidify to liberate the acid.
  • Count carbons first in every synthesis — it tells you whether you need a Special/Grignard step.
  • Planar C=O / carbocation → racemic mixture whenever a new chiral centre forms.
  • A 3° alcohol needs Grignard + ketone — reduction can't do it.
  • Overall yield = y₁ × y₂ × y₃… — fewer steps = higher yield.
  • Iodoform is both a −1C tool and a test for CH₃CO– / CH₃CH(OH)–.
  • Write reagents AND conditions for every step — marks are split between them.
  • WCH15 Jun 2026 synthesis block: Grignard (Q5a) — CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)MgBr gives a specific alcohol, wrong Grignard → wrong product; overall yield 57% (Q11b) — multiply step yields, don't quote step 1. Revise the Grignard + yield chain.
