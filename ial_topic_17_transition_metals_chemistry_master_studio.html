Spec 17.10 – 17.12 Coordination & monodentates

Coordination number & monodentate ligands

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Define coordination number: total number of dative (coordinate) covalent bonds formed with the central metal ion.
  • Classify monodentate ligands: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{OH}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$ (donate 1 lone pair).
  • Neutral ligands ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$) do not change complex charge; anionic ligands ($\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{OH}^-$) alter overall charge.
  • Explain colour changes from changes in oxidation state, ligand type, or coordination number (17.11).
  • Deduce oxidation state: $x + \sum(\text{ligand charges}) = \text{overall charge}$.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Coordination number is the number of DATIVE BONDS, NOT the number of ligand molecules! E.g. $[\text{Cr}(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$ has 3 ligands but coordination number 6 because ethanedioate is bidentate.

Spec 17.13 – 17.15 Shapes & cisplatin

3D complex geometries & cisplatin isomerism

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Octahedral (CN=6): small ligands $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$ ($90^\circ$).
  • Tetrahedral (CN=4): larger, bulky ligands such as $\text{Cl}^-$ due to steric repulsion ($109.5^\circ$).
  • Square planar (CN=4): $\text{Pt}^{2+}$ complexes ($90^\circ$).
  • Linear (CN=2): $\text{Ag}^+$ complexes, e.g. $[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+$, $[\text{Ag}(\text{CN})_2]^{-}$.
  • Cisplatin $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2]$: anticancer drug supplied as a single cis-isomer to bind DNA; the trans-isomer is ineffective and toxic.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Replacing $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with $\text{Cl}^-$ changes coordination number 6 → 4 ($[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} \rightarrow [\text{CuCl}_4]^{2-}$). State that $\text{Cl}^-$ is a larger ligand, so fewer fit around the central metal ion!

Spec 17.16 – 17.17 Chelates & haemoglobin

Bidentate/hexadentate chelates & haemoglobin

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Bidentate ligands: 2 dative bonds per ligand (1,2-diaminoethane en, ethanedioate $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$).
  • Hexadentate: $\text{EDTA}^{4-}$ forms 6 dative bonds ($2 \times \text{:N}$, $4 \times \text{:O}^-$).
  • Chelate effect: monodentate → multidentate substitution increases entropy ($\Delta S_\text{system} > 0$) → more stable complexes.
  • Haemoglobin: $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ in a multidentate porphyrin ring bound to globin protein, leaving 1 site for reversible $\text{O}_2$ binding.
  • CO toxicity: CO forms a much stronger dative covalent bond with $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, replacing $\text{O}_2$ irreversibly.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Always explain stability via total entropy ($\Delta S_\text{total} = \Delta S_\text{system} + \Delta S_\text{surroundings}$). Product moles > reactant moles → $\Delta S_\text{system} > 0$; bond enthalpies similar so $\Delta H \approx 0 \Rightarrow \Delta S_\text{surroundings} \approx 0$.

Spec 17.22 – 17.23 The #1 misconception

Deprotonation vs ligand exchange

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Stage 1 — deprotonation: small amount of NaOH/NH₃ acts as a base, removing H⁺ from water ligands → neutral precipitate: $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow [\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4(\text{OH})_2](\text{s}) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  • Stage 2 — ligand exchange: excess NH₃ substitutes ligands: $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4(\text{OH})_2] + 4\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow [\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_4(\text{H}_2\text{O})_2]^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  • Write the analogous ionic equations for all 8 metal ions (Tab 2 matrix).
  • Identify amphoteric behaviour: Cr³⁺ and Zn²⁺ hydroxides redissolve in excess NaOH.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

⚠️ $\text{OH}^-$ ions DO NOT replace water ligands in stage 1 — they remove protons. Saying “OH⁻ substitutes a water ligand” loses the mark. Stage 2 observation: precipitate dissolves to a deep blue solution.

Spec 17.20 – 17.21 Chromium redox

Chromium(VI) interconversions

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Reduction of $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ by zinc in acidic conditions: +6 → +3 (green) → +2 (sky-blue).
  • Oxidation of Cr³⁺ by hydrogen peroxide in alkaline conditions (then acidification): +3 → +6 (yellow chromate).
  • The acid–base equilibrium: $2\text{CrO}_4^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ — yellow ⇄ orange.
  • Link each step to its observation (Tab 3 cycle).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

The chromate/dichromate equilibrium is not redox (Cr stays +6) — acid just shifts the position. Reduction to Cr²⁺ needs the full Zn/acid treatment; stopping at Cr³⁺ is the common exam stage.

Spec 17.24 Ligand exchange systems

Three exchanges with colour changes

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • i. $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Cu}(\text{OH})_2(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4 \rightarrow [\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_4(\text{H}_2\text{O})_2]^{2+}$ — pale blue → blue ppt → deep royal blue.
  • ii. $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} \rightarrow [\text{CuCl}_4]^{2-}$ with conc. HCl — pale blue → green mixture → yellow; CN 6 → 4 (tetrahedral).
  • iii. $[\text{Co}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} \rightarrow [\text{CoCl}_4]^{2-}$ — pink → blue tetrahedral.
  • Explain the CN change with ligand size (steric repulsion).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

The green stage with conc. HCl is a physical mixture of blue + yellow species, not a new complex — a favourite explanation mark. Write the exchange as an equilibrium ($\rightleftharpoons$).

Spec 17.25 Chelate stability

Why multidentate complexes are more stable

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Count particles: e.g. $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} + \text{EDTA}^{4-} \rightarrow [\text{Cu}(\text{EDTA})]^{2-} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ — 2 → 7 moles.
  • More product moles → $\Delta S_\text{system}$ strongly positive.
  • $\Delta H \approx 0$ (similar dative bonds broken/formed) → $\Delta S_\text{total}$ strongly positive → very stable complex.
  • Apply to en (bidentate) and EDTA⁴⁻ (hexadentate) substitutions (Tab 7 calculator).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

The 4-mark template: (1) mole increase, (2) $\Delta S_\text{system} > 0$, (3) $\Delta H \approx 0$ with reason, (4) therefore $\Delta S_\text{total} > 0$. Memorise the writing template in Tab 7.

Spec 17.26 – 17.29 Heterogeneous catalysis

Surface catalysis, Contact process & converters

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Heterogeneous catalyst = different phase; reaction occurs at surface active sites.
  • Three stages: adsorption (bonds weaken, local proximity) → reaction (lower $E_a$) → desorption (products leave, sites freed).
  • $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ in the Contact process: $\text{SO}_2 + \text{V}_2\text{O}_5 \rightarrow \text{SO}_3 + \text{V}_2\text{O}_4$ then $2\text{V}_2\text{O}_4 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ (V⁵⁺ ⇄ V⁴⁺).
  • Catalytic converter (Pt/Pd/Rh on ceramic honeycomb): $2\text{CO} + 2\text{NO} \xrightarrow{\text{Pt}} 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{N}_2$ — adsorption weakens CO/NO bonds; CO₂ and N₂ desorb.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Exam answers need the adsorption → reaction → desorption sequence with “weakening of bonds” at adsorption. For V₂O₅, show the oxidation-number change of vanadium in both steps.

Spec 17.30 – 17.32 Homogeneous & auto

Homogeneous catalysis & autocatalysis

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Homogeneous catalyst = same phase; reaction proceeds via an intermediate species.
  • Fe²⁺ catalyses $\text{S}_2\text{O}_8^{2-} + 2\text{I}^-$: both reactants are negative (repel, high $E_a$); Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ shuttles via positive–negative steps and is regenerated.
  • Autocatalysis: product Mn²⁺ catalyses $2\text{MnO}_4^- + 5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} + 16\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 2\text{Mn}^{2+} + 10\text{CO}_2 + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  • Sigmoidal rate curve: slow start (anion–anion repulsion) → acceleration as Mn²⁺ builds up → slows as reactants are consumed.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Write the two Mn²⁺ cycle steps: $4\text{Mn}^{2+} + \text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 5\text{Mn}^{3+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ then $2\text{Mn}^{3+} + \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{Mn}^{2+}$. “Autocatalysis = product is the catalyst” is the definition mark.

Aqua-ion acidity M³⁺ vs M²⁺

Hydrolysis acidity of aqua-ions

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • $\text{M}^{3+}$ aqua-ions (e.g. $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$): $[\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons [\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{OH})]^{2+} + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ — noticeably acidic, pH ≈ 2–3.
  • $\text{M}^{2+}$ aqua-ions: only weakly acidic, pH ≈ 5–6.
  • Explain via charge density / polarizing power: M³⁺ pulls electron density from the O–H bonds of water ligands, releasing H⁺.
  • Key comparison: pH of FeCl₃ < pH of FeCl₂ (same concentration).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

The 3-mark explanation: higher charge density → greater polarizing power → O–H bonds weakened, H⁺ released. “More acidic” alone scores nothing.

a. Hydrolysis Acidity Comparison ($\text{M}^{3+}$ vs $\text{M}^{2+}$ Aqua-Ions)

Key Concept: $\text{pH: } \text{FeCl}_3 < \text{FeCl}_2$
$\text{M}^{3+}$ Aqua-Ions (e.g. $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$, $[\text{Cr}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$):
$[\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightleftharpoons [\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{OH})]^{2+}(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{aq})$

Noticeably more acidic (pH ≈ 2–3). High charge density / charge-to-size ratio gives strong polarizing power. This pulls electron density from O–H bonds in water ligands, releasing H⁺ protons into solution.

$\text{M}^{2+}$ Aqua-Ions (e.g. $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$, $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$):
$[\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightleftharpoons [\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{OH})]^+(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{aq})$

Only weakly acidic (pH ≈ 5–6). Lower charge density results in weaker polarizing power, so water ligands retain their H⁺ protons much more strongly.

Thermodynamic Physics & Biochemistry · Spec 17.16 – 17.17, 17.25

Complex Ion Stability, Chelate Effect & Haemoglobin

Understand why multidentate ligands ($\text{en}, \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}, \text{EDTA}^{4-}$) replace monodentate ligands driven by entropy ($\Delta S_{\text{system}} > 0$), and how CO poisons the haem Fe²⁺ site.

Select Ligand Substitution System:

$[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_6]^{2+} + 3\text{en} \rightarrow [\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{2+} + 6\text{NH}_3$
Mole Count & Entropy Analysis:
Reactant Moles 4 moles 1 [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 3 en
Product Moles 7 moles 1 [Co(en)₃]²⁺ + 6 NH₃
Official IAL Thermodynamic Explanation:
  • Entropy of System ($\Delta S_{\text{system}}$): Positive ($\Delta S_{\text{system}} > 0$) because number of moles increases ($\Delta n > 0$), creating significantly more disorder.
  • Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H$): Close to zero ($\Delta H \approx 0$) because the number and strength of dative bonds broken and formed are virtually identical.
  • Total Entropy ($\Delta S_{\text{total}}$): Strongly positive ($\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \frac{-\Delta H}{T} > 0$), making the chelate complex exceptionally stable!

Practical Applications of EDTA & Chelate Complexes

1. Heavy Metal Poisoning

$\text{EDTA}^{4-}$ binds poisonous heavy metal ions (e.g., $\text{Pb}^{2+}$, $\text{Hg}^{2+}$) in blood to form stable, non-toxic soluble chelate complexes excreted in urine.

2. Hard Water Softening

Added to shampoos and detergents to sequester $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions, preventing scum formation and allowing lathering.

📝 Chelate Effect Exam Writing Template:

"The reaction has an increase in entropy ($\Delta S_{\text{system}} > 0$) because the number of moles increases from 4 to 7. The enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) is close to zero because the number and types of dative bonds are similar. Therefore, $\Delta S_{\text{total}}$ is positive, making the complex formed very stable."

Biochemical Ligand Substitution

Haemoglobin Oxygen Transport & Carbon Monoxide Toxicity

Explore the structure of Haem ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$ porphyrin ring) and learn why $\text{CO}$ acts as a competitive, irreversible ligand.

Haem Group ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$ Coordination Architecture)

Ligand Exchange Mechanics in Haemoglobin

1. Normal Oxygen Transport (Oxyhaemoglobin)

Oxygen forms a weak, reversible dative bond with $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ in the lungs where $\text{O}_2$ concentration is high. It travels through blood and releases $\text{O}_2$ readily to respiring tissues.

$\text{haemoglobin} + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{oxyhaemoglobin}$
📝 Official IAL Mark Scheme Explanation for CO Poisoning:

"Carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$) forms a much stronger dative covalent bond with the central $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion than oxygen ($\text{O}_2$). In a ligand exchange reaction, $\text{CO}$ replaces $\text{O}_2$ to form carboxyhaemoglobin. Because this bond is extremely strong and non-reversible, $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ can no longer transport oxygen to cells, leading to asphyxiation."

Heterogeneous, Contact Process, Catalytic Converters & Autocatalysis

Examine surface active site mechanisms, industrial sulfuric acid manufacture, car exhaust conversion, and sigmoidal rate curves.

a. Heterogeneous Catalysis Mechanics (Surface Active Sites)

A solid catalyst operates via 3 sequential stages on its surface:

Stage 1: Adsorption

Reactants bond to active sites on the catalyst surface. This increases local proximity of molecules and weakens covalent bonds within reactants.

Stage 2: Reaction

Adsorbed molecules react on the surface with significantly lower activation energy ($E_a$).

Stage 3: Desorption

Product molecules detach/desorb from active sites, freeing the catalyst surface to adsorb new reactant molecules.

Industrial Process

Manufacture of Conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (Contact Process)

All 4 Chemical Equations:

Step 1 (Combustion): $\text{S}(\text{s}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow \text{SO}_2(\text{g})$

Step 2 (Contact Reaction): $2\text{SO}_2(\text{g}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \xrightleftharpoons[\text{V}_2\text{O}_5, 450^\circ\text{C}]{} 2\text{SO}_3(\text{g})$

Step 3 (Oleum Formation): $\text{SO}_3(\text{g}) + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{l}) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7(\text{l})$

Step 4 (Hydration to Acid): $\text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7(\text{l}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{l})$

$\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ Catalyst Mechanism:

1. $\text{SO}_2 + \text{V}_2\text{O}_5 \rightarrow \text{SO}_3 + \text{V}_2\text{O}_4$ ($\text{V}^{5+} \rightarrow \text{V}^{4+}$)
2. $2\text{V}_2\text{O}_4 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ ($\text{V}^{4+} \rightarrow \text{V}^{5+}$)

Autocatalysis Masterclass

Ethanedioate Titration ($\text{MnO}_4^- + \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$ in Acid)

Autocatalysis: The product of the reaction ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$) acts as the catalyst for the reaction!

Overall Reaction & Why Initial Rate is Slow:
$2\text{MnO}_4^- + 5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} + 16\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 2\text{Mn}^{2+} + 10\text{CO}_2 + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Why slow initially? Both $\text{MnO}_4^-$ and $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$ are negatively charged ions. They strongly repel each other, resulting in a very high activation energy barrier without catalyst!

Intermediate Reactions ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$ Catalytic Cycle):

Step 1: $4\text{Mn}^{2+} + \text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 5\text{Mn}^{3+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Step 2: $2\text{Mn}^{3+} + \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{Mn}^{2+}$

$\text{Mn}^{2+}$ reduces $\text{MnO}_4^-$ to $\text{Mn}^{3+}$, which then oxidizes ethanedioate $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$ back to $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ and $\text{CO}_2$.

Sigmoidal Rate Curve Breakdown:

1. Slow Start: No catalyst ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$) present initially; high anion repulsion barrier.

2. Rapid Acceleration: As $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ forms, it catalyzes the reaction, rapidly speeding up reactant consumption.

3. Slowing Down & Stopping: Reactants ($\text{MnO}_4^-$ and $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$) are consumed, so rate drops to zero.

▸ ⚡ Catalysis one-pager (17.26–17.32)

Heterogeneous: adsorption (weaken bonds) → reaction (lower Eₐ) → desorption. V₂O₅: V⁵⁺→V⁴⁺ then V⁴⁺→V⁵⁺. Converter: Pt/Pd/Rh honeycomb; $2\text{CO} + 2\text{NO} \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{N}_2$.

Homogeneous: same phase, intermediate species. Fe²⁺ shuttles S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ (both negative → repel) via Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺.

Autocatalysis: product Mn²⁺ catalyses MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻ → sigmoidal curve (slow → fast → slows).

▸ ⚠️ Master trap checklist (last-minute)
  • Coordination number counts dative bonds, not ligands (ethanedioate = 2 per ligand).
  • Stage 1 with NaOH/NH₃ is deprotonation — OH⁻ removes H⁺, it does not substitute water.
  • Only Cr³⁺ and Zn²⁺ hydroxides are amphoteric (dissolve in excess NaOH); Fe/Mn/Co/Ni/Cu do not.
  • Excess NH₃ dissolves only Cr³⁺ (purple), Co²⁺ (straw), Ni²⁺ (blue), Cu²⁺ (royal blue), Zn²⁺ (colourless).
  • Green with conc. HCl = mixture of blue + yellow, not a new complex; CN drops 6 → 4 because Cl⁻ is larger.
  • Chromate ⇄ dichromate is an acid–base equilibrium, not redox (Cr stays +6).
  • Cu⁺ disproportionates in water: $2\text{Cu}^+ \rightarrow \text{Cu} + \text{Cu}^{2+}$.
  • Chelate stability = entropy (mole increase), not stronger bonds (ΔH ≈ 0).
  • CO toxicity: stronger dative bond, irreversible, carboxyhaemoglobin — never “CO blocks the lungs”.
  • Heterogeneous mechanism needs all three stages: adsorption (bond weakening) → reaction → desorption.
  • Autocatalysis: the product is the catalyst; sigmoidal curve; slow start = anion–anion repulsion.
  • M³⁺ acidity: charge density → polarizing power → O–H weakened → H⁺ released (pH 2–3 vs 5–6).
