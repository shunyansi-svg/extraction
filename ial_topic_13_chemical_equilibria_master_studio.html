Topic 13 Learning Points & Mark Scheme Secrets (Spec 13.1 – 13.9)

Essential learning goals and examiner watch-outs across Chemical Equilibria. Builds on Topic 9B (Unit 2): dynamic equilibrium and Le Chatelier's principle are assumed.

Spec 13.1 $K_c$ expressions

1. Writing $K_c$ Expressions — Homogeneous & Heterogeneous

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • For $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$, write $K_c = \dfrac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b}$ — products over reactants.
  • Use equilibrium concentrations in $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$, each raised to its balancing number.
  • Homogeneous (all aq or all g): include every species.
  • Heterogeneous: omit pure solids and pure liquids (their concentrations are constant).
  • Use square brackets $[\,]$ for concentration — never round brackets.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Round brackets or partial pressures in a $K_c$ expression are not awarded. For $\text{CaCO}_3(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)$, $K_c = [\text{CO}_2]$ only — both solids are omitted.

Spec 13.2 $K_p$ expressions

2. Writing $K_p$ Expressions — Partial Pressures in atm

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • $K_p$ applies to gaseous equilibria: $K_p = \dfrac{(p_C)^c(p_D)^d}{(p_A)^a(p_B)^b}$.
  • Partial pressures are in atm; write $p_{\text{X}}$ (or $p(\text{X})$) for each gas.
  • Compute partial pressure as $p_{\text{X}} = x_{\text{X}} \times P_{total}$.
  • Mole fraction $x_{\text{X}} = \dfrac{\text{moles of X}}{\text{total moles of gas}}$.
  • In heterogeneous equilibria include gases only — omit solids and liquids.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Don't use concentrations in $K_p$ — only partial pressures. For $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3(g) + \text{HCl}(g)$, $K_p = p_{\text{NH}_3} \times p_{\text{HCl}}$ (the solid is omitted).

Spec 13.3 Calculating $K_c$ & $K_p$

3. Calculating $K_c$ & $K_p$ from Experimental Data

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Use an ICE table (Initial, Change, Equilibrium) to find equilibrium moles.
  • For $K_c$: divide equilibrium moles by the vessel volume ($\text{dm}^3$) to get concentrations.
  • Substitute into the expression and evaluate the number.
  • For $K_p$: find total moles → mole fractions → partial pressures ($x \times P$).
  • Work out and state the units (they change with the expression).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

The full step-by-step method is on the Kc/Kp Method tab. The two most lost marks are using moles instead of concentration and wrong/missing units.

Spec 13.4 Le Chatelier factors

4. Effect of Temperature, Pressure & Catalyst on Composition

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • State Le Chatelier's principle: the system shifts to oppose the change.
  • Concentration: adding a reactant shifts the position to the product side.
  • Pressure (gases): increasing $P$ shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas.
  • Catalyst: no shift — speeds forward and reverse equally, reaching equilibrium faster.
  • Apply to both homogeneous and heterogeneous systems.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Pressure only matters when the number of gas moles differs between sides, and only for gases. In the Haber process ($4 \to 2$ mol gas) high pressure favours $\text{NH}_3$.

Spec 13.5 What does NOT change $K$

5. $K$ Is Unchanged by Concentration, Pressure & Catalyst

🎯 4 Key Learning Goals:
  • The equilibrium constant changes with temperature only.
  • Changing concentration or pressure shifts the position, but $K$ stays the same.
  • A catalyst has no effect on $K$ or on the position.
  • Distinguish clearly between "position of equilibrium" and "value of $K$".
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

"The catalyst increases the yield / increases $K$" is always wrong. After a concentration or pressure change, the system moves until the same $K$ is restored.

Spec 13.6 – 13.7 Temperature & $K$

6. Temperature Changes the Value of $K$

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Exothermic forward reaction: raising $T$ decreases $K$ (shifts left).
  • Endothermic forward reaction: raising $T$ increases $K$ (shifts right).
  • Lowering $T$ does the opposite in each case.
  • Explain the temperature effect on position by stating that $K$ itself changes.
  • Apply to both $K_c$ and $K_p$.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

For full marks say: "temperature changes the value of $K$, so the position shifts" — not just "the position shifts". Haber is exothermic ($\Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$), so high $T$ lowers $K$.

Spec 13.8 $\Delta S_{total} = R\ln K$

7. Linking Entropy and the Equilibrium Constant

🎯 4 Key Learning Goals:
  • Know the relationship $\Delta S_{total} = R \ln K$.
  • Explain that temperature changes $\Delta S_{surroundings} = -\Delta H/T$, hence $\Delta S_{total}$, hence $K$.
  • For an exothermic reaction, raising $T$ makes $\Delta S_{total}$ less positive, so $K$ falls.
  • Relate a larger $K$ to a more positive $\Delta S_{total}$.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

This is the why behind the temperature effect. Use $T$ in kelvin. This links Topic 12 (entropy) to Topic 13 — a favourite cross-topic exam angle.

Spec 13.9 Extent of reaction

8. Using $K$ to Predict How Far a Reaction Goes

🎯 4 Key Learning Goals:
  • Large $K$ ($\gg 1$): equilibrium lies to the right — products dominate, reaction nearly complete.
  • Small $K$ ($\ll 1$): equilibrium lies to the left — reactants dominate, little reaction.
  • $K \approx 1$: significant amounts of both sides present.
  • Compare $K$ values to judge relative extents of different reactions.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

$K$ tells you the extent, not the speed. A large $K$ does not mean the reaction is fast — kinetics (Topic 11) controls the rate.

Assumed prior knowledge (Topic 9B, Unit 2): dynamic equilibrium, closed systems, and the qualitative Le Chatelier principle. Not covered here: acid–base equilibria, pH, buffers and titration curves (see the Unit 14 acid–base studio), and the Haber-process industrial conditions discussion (Unit 5 / Topic 9B context).

$K_c$ — Expressions & Concepts (Spec 13.1, 13.3)

6 concepts mapped
1. What is $K_c$?
$aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD \qquad K_c = \dfrac{[C]^c[D]^d}{[A]^a[B]^b}$

The equilibrium constant in terms of concentration. Products over reactants, each raised to its balancing number, using equilibrium concentrations in $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$. At a given temperature $K_c$ is constant.

Learning goals
  • Write the general $K_c$ expression from any balanced equation.
  • State that concentrations are equilibrium values.
Examiner tips
  • Products on top — inverting the fraction loses all the marks.
  • Use square brackets, not round.
2. Homogeneous $K_c$ (all one phase)
$\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3 \qquad K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NH}_3]^2}{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3}$

When all species are in the same phase (all gas, or all aqueous), every species appears in the expression. Another example: $\text{Fe}^{3+}(aq) + \text{SCN}^-(aq) \rightleftharpoons \text{FeSCN}^{2+}(aq)$ gives $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{FeSCN}^{2+}]}{[\text{Fe}^{3+}][\text{SCN}^-]}$.

Learning goals
  • Include all gaseous or aqueous species.
  • Raise each to its coefficient.
Examiner tips
  • The Haber expression is examined repeatedly — memorise it.
3. Heterogeneous $K_c$ (omit solids & liquids)
$\text{CaCO}_3(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g) \qquad K_c = [\text{CO}_2]$

Pure solids and pure liquids have constant concentrations, so they are left out. Only gases and aqueous species appear. For $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3(g) + \text{HCl}(g)$, $K_c = [\text{NH}_3][\text{HCl}]$.

Learning goals
  • Identify and omit pure solids/liquids.
  • Write the reduced expression correctly.
Examiner tips
  • Including $[\text{CaCO}_3]$ or $[\text{CaO}]$ is a standard lost mark.
4. Units of $K_c$
$\text{Haber: } \dfrac{(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^2}{(\text{mol dm}^{-3})(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^3} = \text{mol}^{-2}\text{ dm}^{6}$

Substitute $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ for every concentration term and simplify. Units differ for each expression. If the powers cancel exactly (e.g. $\text{H}_2 + \text{I}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$), $K_c$ has no units.

Learning goals
  • Derive units for any $K_c$ expression.
  • Recognise when $K_c$ is dimensionless.
Examiner tips
  • Units are usually worth a mark — never omit them unless they cancel.
5. Substituting equilibrium concentrations
$K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NH}_3]^2}{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3} = \dfrac{(0.40)^2}{(0.20)(0.60)^3} = 3.7\ \text{mol}^{-2}\text{ dm}^{6}$

Insert the equilibrium concentrations (not initial, not moles). Square/cube as the expression demands, then evaluate. The full ICE-table method is on the Kc/Kp Method tab.

Learning goals
  • Substitute and evaluate with a calculator.
  • Keep units throughout.
Examiner tips
  • Apply the power to the whole concentration: $[\text{H}_2]^3$, not $[\text{H}_2 \times 3]$.
6. What the size of $K_c$ tells you
$K_c \gg 1 \rightarrow \text{products favoured}\qquad K_c \ll 1 \rightarrow \text{reactants favoured}$

A large $K_c$ means the equilibrium mixture is mostly products (reaction goes nearly to completion). A small $K_c$ means mostly reactants (little reaction). $K_c \approx 1$ means both sides are significant.

Learning goals
  • Interpret the magnitude of $K_c$.
  • Compare extents of different reactions.
Examiner tips
  • $K_c$ says nothing about rate — a large $K_c$ can still be a slow reaction.

⚡ High-Yield Trap Pairs — $K_c$
Trap 1: Products/Reactants the right way

Products on top, reactants below. Inverting the fraction is the single most common $K_c$ error.

Trap 2: Powers vs multiplying

$[\text{H}_2]^3$ means concentration cubed. It is not $3 \times [\text{H}_2]$. Apply the coefficient as an exponent.

Trap 3: Solids in heterogeneous $K_c$

Pure solids and liquids are omitted. Only (g) and (aq) appear. Their "concentration" is constant.

Trap 4: Brackets

Use square brackets $[\text{NH}_3]$ for concentration. Round brackets or partial pressures in a $K_c$ answer score zero.

$K_p$ — Partial Pressures & Mole Fractions (Spec 13.2, 13.3)

6 concepts mapped
1. What is $K_p$?
$\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3 \qquad K_p = \dfrac{(p_{\text{NH}_3})^2}{p_{\text{N}_2}\,(p_{\text{H}_2})^3}$

The equilibrium constant in terms of partial pressures, used for gaseous equilibria. Pressures in atm. Products over reactants, each raised to its balancing number, using equilibrium partial pressures.

Learning goals
  • Write a $K_p$ expression for any gaseous equilibrium.
  • State that pressures are in atm.
Examiner tips
  • Never put concentrations in $K_p$ — only partial pressures.
2. Mole fraction, $x$
$x_{\text{X}} = \dfrac{\text{moles of X at equilibrium}}{\text{total moles of gas at equilibrium}}$

The fraction of all gas molecules that are X. Mole fractions have no units and always sum to 1. You need the equilibrium moles of every gas to find them.

Learning goals
  • Calculate mole fractions from equilibrium moles.
  • Check they sum to 1.
Examiner tips
  • Use total equilibrium moles, not initial moles, in the denominator.
3. Partial pressure, $p_{\text{X}} = x_{\text{X}} \times P$
$p_{\text{X}} = x_{\text{X}} \times P_{total}$

Each gas contributes a share of the total pressure proportional to its mole fraction. The partial pressures sum to $P_{total}$. This is the bridge from moles to the $K_p$ expression.

Learning goals
  • Convert mole fraction + total pressure to partial pressure.
  • Verify partial pressures sum to $P_{total}$.
Examiner tips
  • Forgetting to multiply by $P_{total}$ (using mole fractions as pressures) is a classic error.
4. Heterogeneous $K_p$ — gases only
$\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3(g) + \text{HCl}(g) \qquad K_p = p_{\text{NH}_3} \times p_{\text{HCl}}$

Only gaseous species appear in $K_p$. Solids and liquids are omitted entirely. Here the solid $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ does not appear, and there is no denominator.

Learning goals
  • Omit solids/liquids from $K_p$.
  • Handle expressions with no denominator.
Examiner tips
  • For $\text{C}(s)+\text{CO}_2(g)\rightleftharpoons 2\text{CO}(g)$, $K_p = (p_{\text{CO}})^2 / p_{\text{CO}_2}$ — carbon is omitted.
5. Units of $K_p$
$\text{Haber: } \dfrac{\text{atm}^2}{\text{atm}\times\text{atm}^3} = \text{atm}^{-2}$

Substitute atm for every partial-pressure term and simplify. For $\text{H}_2+\text{I}_2\rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$ the units cancel → $K_p$ is dimensionless. For $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ decomposition, units are $\text{atm}^2$.

Learning goals
  • Derive $K_p$ units for any expression.
  • Recognise dimensionless cases.
Examiner tips
  • Negative powers of atm (e.g. $\text{atm}^{-2}$) are expected — don't "fix" them.
6. $K_p$ calculation — the route
$\text{moles} \rightarrow \text{total moles} \rightarrow x \rightarrow p = xP \rightarrow K_p$

From an ICE table get equilibrium moles → sum to total moles → divide each by the total for mole fractions → multiply by $P_{total}$ for partial pressures → substitute into $K_p$. Try it live in the $K_p$ Calculator (Module 2).

Learning goals
  • Follow the full mole → $K_p$ pipeline.
  • Keep track of units (atm).
Examiner tips
  • Show each stage — method marks are awarded for mole fractions and partial pressures even if the final $K_p$ slips.

⚡ High-Yield Trap Pairs — $K_p$
Trap 1: Mole fraction ≠ partial pressure

You must multiply the mole fraction by $P_{total}$. Substituting mole fractions straight into $K_p$ gives a wrong number and wrong units.

Trap 2: Initial vs equilibrium moles

Mole fractions use equilibrium moles. Using the starting moles is a very common slip — build the ICE table first.

Trap 3: Concentrations in $K_p$

$K_p$ takes partial pressures only. If you're given concentrations for a gas, convert via the ideal-gas route or use $K_c$ instead.

Trap 4: Total moles in the denominator

The mole-fraction denominator is the sum of all gaseous moles at equilibrium — forgetting a species (e.g. an inert gas) skews every partial pressure.

Le Chatelier's Principle & What Changes $K$ (Spec 13.4 – 13.8)

6 concepts mapped
1. Le Chatelier's principle
$\text{If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, the position shifts to oppose the change.}$

Applies to changes in concentration, pressure and temperature. It predicts the direction of the shift. Remember it governs the position; only temperature changes the constant $K$ itself.

Learning goals
  • State the principle in exam wording.
  • Apply it to predict shift direction.
Examiner tips
  • Use the word "oppose" (or "counteract") — it's the key term.
2. Concentration changes
$\text{add reactant} \rightarrow \text{shifts right}\qquad \text{remove product} \rightarrow \text{shifts right}$

Adding a reactant shifts the position to the product side to use it up; removing a product also shifts right. The system moves until the same $K$ is restored — $K$ does not change.

Learning goals
  • Predict the shift for adding/removing any species.
  • State that $K$ is unchanged.
Examiner tips
  • In the Haber process, continuously removing $\text{NH}_3$ keeps driving the position right — an industrial yield trick.
3. Pressure changes (gases only)
$\uparrow P \rightarrow \text{shifts to side with fewer gas moles}$

Increasing pressure (reducing volume) shifts the position to the side with fewer moles of gas. For Haber ($4 \to 2$ mol gas) high pressure favours $\text{NH}_3$. If gas moles are equal on both sides, pressure has no effect on position. $K$ is unchanged.

Learning goals
  • Count gas moles on each side and predict the shift.
  • Recognise the no-effect case ($\Delta n_{gas}=0$).
Examiner tips
  • Only count gaseous moles — ignore solids, liquids and aqueous species.
4. Catalyst — no shift, no change in $K$
$\text{catalyst: faster both ways} \rightarrow \text{equilibrium reached sooner, same position}$

A catalyst lowers $E_a$ for the forward and reverse reactions equally. It speeds up attainment of equilibrium but does not shift the position or change $K$. The yield is unchanged.

Learning goals
  • State that a catalyst affects rate only.
  • Explain equal forward/reverse acceleration.
Examiner tips
  • "Catalyst increases yield/$K$" is always wrong — a guaranteed lost mark if written.
5. Temperature is the only thing that changes $K$
$\text{exothermic: } \uparrow T \Rightarrow K\downarrow\ (\text{shifts left})\qquad \text{endothermic: } \uparrow T \Rightarrow K\uparrow\ (\text{shifts right})$

Raising temperature shifts the position in the endothermic direction and changes the value of $K$. For an exothermic forward reaction, $K$ falls as $T$ rises. The position shift is explained by the change in $K$ (Spec 13.7).

Learning goals
  • Link the sign of $\Delta H$ to how $K$ responds to $T$.
  • Explain the position shift via the change in $K$.
Examiner tips
  • Haber is exothermic — high $T$ lowers $K$ and the yield, so industry compromises at ~450 °C for a reasonable rate.
6. Why temperature changes $K$: $\Delta S_{total} = R\ln K$
$\Delta S_{total} = R \ln K \qquad \Delta S_{surroundings} = -\dfrac{\Delta H}{T}$

Temperature changes $\Delta S_{surroundings}$ (since it is $-\Delta H/T$), which changes $\Delta S_{total}$, which changes $K$ via $\Delta S_{total}=R\ln K$. For an exothermic reaction, raising $T$ makes $\Delta S_{total}$ less positive, so $K$ decreases.

Learning goals
  • State $\Delta S_{total}=R\ln K$ and use it to justify the $T$ effect.
  • Connect Topic 12 (entropy) to Topic 13.
Examiner tips
  • This is the high-mark "explain" route. Use $T$ in kelvin.

⚡ High-Yield Trap Pairs — Le Chatelier
Trap 1: Position vs $K$

Concentration and pressure shift the position but leave $K$ unchanged. Only temperature changes $K$. Keep these two ideas separate in every answer.

Trap 2: Catalyst myths

A catalyst never shifts the position, changes $K$, or raises the yield. It only speeds up reaching the same equilibrium.

Trap 3: Temperature direction

Exothermic forward: heat up → $K$ falls, shifts left. Students often write the opposite. Anchor it: heat favours the endothermic direction.

Trap 4: Pressure with equal moles

If gas moles are equal on both sides (e.g. $\text{H}_2+\text{I}_2\rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$), pressure changes the position not at all. Count the gas moles first.

The Method · Spec 13.3

How to Calculate $K_c$ and $K_p$ — Step by Step

Two reliable routines built on the ICE table (Initial · Change · Equilibrium). Follow the steps in order and you will pick up every method mark.

🧮 Method A — Calculating $K_c$ (concentrations)
  1. Write the balanced equation. Everything else depends on the balancing numbers.
  2. Write the $K_c$ expression — products over reactants, each raised to its coefficient, square brackets. Omit pure solids/liquids.
  3. Build an ICE table (in moles): Initial, Change, Equilibrium. Use the mole ratios for the Change row.
  4. Convert equilibrium moles to concentration: $[X] = \dfrac{n_X}{V}$ with $V$ in $\text{dm}^3$.
  5. Substitute the equilibrium concentrations into the expression and evaluate.
  6. Work out the units by substituting $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$ for each term and simplifying.
Worked example ($K_c$)

For $\text{N}_2(g) + 3\text{H}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3(g)$, a $1.0\ \text{dm}^3$ vessel at equilibrium contains $0.20\ \text{mol N}_2$, $0.60\ \text{mol H}_2$ and $0.40\ \text{mol NH}_3$. Find $K_c$.

Species$\text{N}_2$$3\text{H}_2$$2\text{NH}_3$
Equilibrium moles0.200.600.40
÷ V = 1.0 dm³ → $[\,]$ (mol dm⁻³)0.200.600.40
$K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NH}_3]^2}{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3} = \dfrac{(0.40)^2}{(0.20)(0.60)^3} = \dfrac{0.16}{0.20 \times 0.216} = \dfrac{0.16}{0.0432} = 3.7$
Units: $\dfrac{(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^2}{(\text{mol dm}^{-3})(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^3} = \text{mol}^{-2}\text{ dm}^{6}$
$\therefore\; K_c = \mathbf{3.7\ \text{mol}^{-2}\text{ dm}^{6}}$
🧮 Method B — Calculating $K_p$ (partial pressures)
  1. Write the balanced equation and the $K_p$ expression (gases only, partial pressures, omit solids/liquids).
  2. ICE table → equilibrium moles of every gas.
  3. Total moles of gas: $n_{total} = \sum n_{gas}$.
  4. Mole fraction of each gas: $x_X = n_X / n_{total}$ (they sum to 1).
  5. Partial pressure: $p_X = x_X \times P_{total}$ (atm).
  6. Substitute into $K_p$, evaluate, and give the units in powers of atm.
Worked example ($K_p$)

For $\text{N}_2(g) + 3\text{H}_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3(g)$, the equilibrium mixture contains $0.50\ \text{mol N}_2$, $1.50\ \text{mol H}_2$ and $1.00\ \text{mol NH}_3$ at a total pressure of $200\ \text{atm}$. Find $K_p$.

$n_{total} = 0.50 + 1.50 + 1.00 = 3.00\ \text{mol}$
$x_{\text{N}_2} = 0.50/3.00 = 0.167 \quad x_{\text{H}_2} = 1.50/3.00 = 0.500 \quad x_{\text{NH}_3} = 1.00/3.00 = 0.333$
$p_{\text{N}_2} = 0.167 \times 200 = 33.3\ \text{atm} \quad p_{\text{H}_2} = 0.500 \times 200 = 100\ \text{atm} \quad p_{\text{NH}_3} = 0.333 \times 200 = 66.7\ \text{atm}$
$K_p = \dfrac{(p_{\text{NH}_3})^2}{p_{\text{N}_2}\,(p_{\text{H}_2})^3} = \dfrac{(66.7)^2}{(33.3)(100)^3} = \dfrac{4449}{33.3 \times 10^{6}} = 1.34 \times 10^{-4}$
Units: $\dfrac{\text{atm}^2}{\text{atm}\times\text{atm}^3} = \text{atm}^{-2}$
$\therefore\; K_p = \mathbf{1.34 \times 10^{-4}\ \text{atm}^{-2}}$
⚠️ Heterogeneous systems — the short version

Omit pure solids and liquids before you substitute. For $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}(s) \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3(g) + \text{HCl}(g)$, $K_p = p_{\text{NH}_3} \times p_{\text{HCl}}$. Because the two gases are produced in a 1:1 ratio, $p_{\text{NH}_3} = p_{\text{HCl}} = P_{total}/2$, so $K_p = (P_{total}/2)^2$. There is no denominator.

If $P_{total} = 0.60\ \text{atm}$: $K_p = (0.30)^2 = 0.09\ \text{atm}^{2}$
Examiner's Error Log

Common Mistakes — and How to Avoid Them

The errors that cost the most marks on $K_c$/$K_p$ and equilibrium questions, each shown as wrong → right.

A · Writing the expression
1. Reactants over products (inverted)

✗ Wrong: $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3}{[\text{NH}_3]^2}$

✓ Right: $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NH}_3]^2}{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3}$ — products always on top.

2. Multiplying instead of raising to a power

✗ Wrong: $[\text{H}_2 \times 3]$ or $3[\text{H}_2]$

✓ Right: $[\text{H}_2]^3$ — the coefficient is an exponent.

3. Including solids / liquids

✗ Wrong: $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{CaO}][\text{CO}_2]}{[\text{CaCO}_3]}$

✓ Right: $K_c = [\text{CO}_2]$ — omit pure solids and liquids.

4. Round brackets or partial pressures in $K_c$

✗ Wrong: $K_c = \dfrac{(\text{NH}_3)^2}{\dots}$ or using $p_{\text{NH}_3}$

✓ Right: square brackets $[\text{NH}_3]$ for concentration. Not awarded otherwise.

B · Substituting the numbers
5. Using moles instead of concentration ($K_c$)

✗ Wrong: plugging $n = 0.40\ \text{mol}$ straight into $K_c$.

✓ Right: divide by the volume first: $[X] = n/V$ in $\text{mol dm}^{-3}$.

6. Using initial (not equilibrium) values

✗ Wrong: substituting the starting moles/concentrations.

✓ Right: only equilibrium values go into $K$ — build the ICE table.

7. Using mole fractions as partial pressures ($K_p$)

✗ Wrong: $K_p = \dfrac{(0.333)^2}{(0.167)(0.500)^3}$ (mole fractions, no $P$).

✓ Right: multiply each by $P_{total}$ first: $p_X = x_X \times P$.

8. Wrong total moles in the mole fraction

✗ Wrong: using initial total moles, or forgetting a gas.

✓ Right: sum all gaseous moles at equilibrium; check $x$ sums to 1.

C · Units
9. Missing or wrong units

✗ Wrong: "$K_c = 3.7$" with no units.

✓ Right: $K_c = 3.7\ \text{mol}^{-2}\text{ dm}^{6}$ — derive them every time.

10. "Fixing" negative powers

✗ Wrong: rewriting $\text{atm}^{-2}$ as $\text{atm}^{2}$.

✓ Right: negative powers are correct (e.g. $K_p$ for Haber is $\text{atm}^{-2}$).

D · Le Chatelier & the constant
11. "Catalyst increases the yield / $K$"

✗ Wrong: a catalyst shifts the position or changes $K$.

✓ Right: it only speeds up reaching the same equilibrium — no change in yield or $K$.

12. "Pressure/concentration changes $K$"

✗ Wrong: increasing pressure changes $K$.

✓ Right: they shift the position only; $K$ changes with temperature alone.

13. Temperature direction reversed

✗ Wrong: "exothermic + heat → $K$ increases".

✓ Right: exothermic forward: heat up → $K$ decreases (shifts left). Heat favours the endothermic side.

14. Confusing rate with extent

✗ Wrong: "large $K_c$ means the reaction is fast".

✓ Right: $K$ tells you the extent; kinetics (Topic 11) controls the rate.

⚠️ Master Trap Checklist cross-cutting ▼
  • Products over reactants — never invert.
  • Coefficients are exponents, not multipliers.
  • Omit pure solids and liquids (both $K_c$ and $K_p$).
  • Square brackets for $K_c$; round brackets score zero.
  • Use equilibrium values, never initial.
  • $K_c$: divide moles by volume first.
  • $K_p$: mole fraction × total pressure — don't skip the ×$P$.
  • Total moles = all gaseous moles at equilibrium.
  • Always give units (they can be negative powers or none).
  • Only temperature changes $K$.
  • Catalyst: faster, same position, same $K$, same yield.
  • Pressure shifts position only when gas moles differ.
  • Exothermic + heat → $K$ falls (shifts left).
  • Explain the $T$ effect by the change in $K$ (Spec 13.7).
  • $\Delta S_{total} = R\ln K$; $T$ in kelvin.
  • $K$ = extent, not rate.
